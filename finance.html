Comparing Greeks Across Credit InstrumentsNew

  • Credit risk
  • Fixed income
  • Python

7 instruments · 6 risk factors · rate DV01 per 100: floater −0.0002, fixed bond −0.0435

Built one survival-discounting framework that prices seven credit instruments side by side: an amortizing auto loan, a prepayable mortgage, a floating-rate term loan, a revolver, a fixed-rate bond, a CDS, and a callable bond. Each instrument is bumped and repriced to produce a common vector of Greeks, covering rate, spread, hazard rate, recovery, prepayment, and rate gamma, all per 100 dollars of notional.

Implemented in Python with a single generic bump-and-reprice engine and a sanity-check suite that asserts known results, such as the CDS par spread matching h(1 − ρ) and the floater carrying near-zero rate duration.

Key findings

  • Floating-rate structure removes rate duration but not spread duration: the term loan's rate exposure is about zero while its spread exposure matches the fixed bond's
  • Mortgages and callable bonds carry negative gamma from embedded options; at −200bp the fixed bond gains 9.1 while the mortgage gains only 1.8
  • Prepayment risk sits only in the amortizing consumer products, and the premium mortgage loses about twice as much per CPR point as the auto loan
  • The CDS buyer mirrors a bondholder on credit, gaining 7.19 at +300bp of hazard while the bond loses 7.32

Polymarket Earnings Signal

  • Prediction markets
  • Event study
  • Python

n = 1,231 contracts · Brier 0.149 vs. 0.185 · encompassing coef. 5.78, p < 10−35

Tested whether Polymarket's recurring "Will [company] beat quarterly earnings?" markets carry information about earnings surprises beyond a firm's own historical beat rate, using 1,231 resolved contracts across roughly 415 tickers. The design builds a no-look-ahead, firm-specific baseline, then runs calibration tests, an encompassing regression, an event study, and a backtested trading strategy with clustering-robust inference.

Key findings

  • Polymarket's implied probability is well calibrated and beats the firm-history baseline (Brier score 0.149 vs. 0.185)
  • Encompassing regression coefficient of 5.78 (p < 10−35), robust to three clustering-robust inference procedures
  • Neither market momentum nor the stock's pre-announcement move adds information beyond the market price
  • A volatility-targeted strategy beats benchmarks in-sample, but permutation, block-bootstrap, and Jobson-Korkie tests cannot separate the edge from noise once same-day clustering cuts the sample to about 181 independent dates

Quantitative Portfolio Management

  • Factor models
  • GARCH
  • Python

Fama-French factor regressions · GARCH variants · index and industry portfolios

Built a quantitative framework to analyze return distributions, factor exposures, and volatility dynamics across market indices and industry portfolios.

Modeled returns using Fama-French factors and estimated volatility with GARCH variants to understand how risk evolves over time. The results show clear evidence of fat tails, volatility clustering, and time-varying risk, highlighting where standard normal assumptions break down in real markets.

Implemented the full pipeline in Python, including data processing, regression modeling, volatility estimation, and visualization.

Key findings

  • Returns show fat tails and negative skew, meaning downside risk is underestimated
  • Volatility is clustered, not constant, which supports GARCH modeling
  • Factor exposure explains most return variation across industries
  • Normal distributions underestimate extreme moves

Nelson-Siegel / Diebold-Li Treasury ETF Rotation Strategy

  • Yield curve
  • VAR
  • Python

Top-3: CAGR 3.84%, Sharpe 0.392, MaxDD −26.55% · Equal weight: CAGR 2.37%, Sharpe 0.290

Built a systematic fixed income strategy to rotate across Treasury ETFs using yield curve mispricing and directional forecasting signals.

Modeled the yield curve using Nelson-Siegel to identify mispricings at each maturity, then applied Diebold-Li dynamic factor forecasting via VAR(1) to predict where the curve's level, slope, and curvature are heading. The two signals are blended monthly to shift allocation toward ETFs that are both currently cheap and directionally favorable.

Implemented the full pipeline in Python, including yield curve fitting, expanding-window VAR estimation, signal construction, portfolio rebalancing, and performance benchmarking against AGG.

Key findings

  • NS residuals reliably identify cheap vs. rich maturity segments each month
  • DL slope factor (β1) is the most forecastable and drives the strongest directional signal
  • Blended strategy outperforms equal-weight benchmark on a risk-adjusted basis across most rate regimes
  • Signal decomposition shows the directional overlay adds value beyond static mispricing alone
